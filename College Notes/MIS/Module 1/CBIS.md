A **Computer-Based Information System (CBIS)** is an organized setup that uses computers, software, networks, and people to collect raw data, process it, store it, and turn it into useful information for running a business and making decisions.

Before computers, businesses used manual information systems—paper registers, filing cabinets, and calculators. A CBIS replaces that slow manual work with computers so information is processed faster, with fewer mistakes, and shared instantly.

### How a CBIS Works (Basic Flow)

Every CBIS follows four simple steps:

1. **Input:** Collects raw data (e.g., scanning a barcode at a store).
    
2. **Processing:** Converts that raw data into something meaningful (e.g., calculating the total bill, tax, and updating stock).
    
3. **Output:** Produces useful information (e.g., printing the receipt and showing daily sales to the manager).
    
4. **Storage & Feedback:** Saves the data for future use and flags issues (e.g., alerting the manager when stock runs low).
    

## The 6 Core Components of a CBIS

To make this easy to remember, imagine a **supermarket billing and inventory system** (like DMart or Reliance Smart) as our running example.

### 1. Hardware (The Physical Equipment)

Hardware includes all the physical, touchable devices used to input, process, store, and output data.

- **General examples:** Computers, servers, keyboards, printers, smartphones, and servers.
    
- **Supermarket example:** The barcode scanner, cash register monitor, receipt printer, card swipe machine, and the back-office server.
    

### 2. Software (The Programs & Instructions)

Software consists of the computer programs that tell the hardware what to do. It includes **system software** (like Windows or Linux) and **application software** (built for specific tasks).

- **General examples:** Operating systems, Excel, Tally, and mobile apps.
    
- **Supermarket example:** The Point-of-Sale (POS) billing app that reads the barcode, looks up the price, applies discounts, and calculates GST.
    

### 3. Database / Data (The Raw Facts)

A database is an organized collection of related data stored electronically so the system can search and update it quickly. Without data, hardware and software have nothing to work on.

- **General examples:** Customer records, employee payroll tables, and product catalogs.
    
- **Supermarket example:** The product database storing every item's name, price, expiry date, and remaining quantity in stock.
    

### 4. Networks & Telecommunications (The Connections)

Networks connect computers and devices together so they can share data and communicate across a room or across the globe.

- **General examples:** Wi-Fi, LAN cables, the Internet, and cloud connections.
    
- **Supermarket example:** The store's local network connecting all 10 billing counters to the central server, plus the internet connection that verifies UPI/credit card payments with the bank in seconds.
    

### 5. People (The Most Important Component)

People are the humans who build, run, maintain, and use the system. Even the best technology is useless without people operating it. This includes **end-users** (everyday workers/customers) and **IT specialists** (developers, database admins, tech support).

- **General examples:** Clerks, managers, customers, and software engineers.
    
- **Supermarket example:** The cashiers scanning items, the store manager checking sales reports, and the IT technician who fixes the system if a server crashes.
    

### 6. Procedures (The Rules & Instructions)

Procedures are the step-by-step rules, policies, and guidelines that tell people how to use the system properly and safely.

- **General examples:** Login security rules, daily backup schedules, and refund policies.
    
- **Supermarket example:** The rule that a cashier must get a manager's password to cancel a bill, or the nightly routine of backing up all sales data at 10 PM.
    

## Common Types of CBIS in a Business

Different levels of employees use different kinds of CBIS:

- **Transaction Processing System (TPS):** Handles day-to-day routine work (e.g., billing customers, processing ATM withdrawals).
    
- **Management Information System (MIS):** Turns TPS data into regular summary reports for middle managers (e.g., monthly sales report by branch).
    
- **Decision Support System (DSS):** Helps managers solve complex, one-off problems using "what-if" analysis (e.g., deciding the best city to open a new store).
    
- **Executive Support System (ESS):** Gives top bosses (CEOs) simple visual dashboards showing big-picture trends and competitor performance.
